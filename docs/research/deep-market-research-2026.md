## Executive Summary

**The world has two venture markets**: a ~$105B normal market and a ~$215B AI overlay. In Q1'26, global VC hit a record $330.9B — but 64% went to just 4 companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Waymo). The real story is underneath: AI is restructuring every sector, and **China is the second ecosystem that matters**.

**China in 2026 is defined by five structural shifts:**

1. **Embodied AI explosion** — $30B funded in Q1'26 alone, 9 unicorns, Shenzhen producing robots at 3-4x the speed of US competitors
2. **AI agents going mainstream** — ¥449B ($62B) market in 2026, growing 107% YoY; Politburo explicitly endorsed "AI+ action" and government procurement of agent services
3. **China-to-Global model** — 86% of successful Chinese AI startups now target overseas markets; USD funds investing aggressively in Chinese teams building for global users
4. **State capital reshaping the stack** — ¥1T ($138B) National VC Guidance Fund targeting hard tech; Beijing launched ¥20B four-fund structure for RISC-V + AI + robotics + biotech
5. **Regulatory tightening** — Manus/MiroMind cases show Beijing won't let AI startups "Singapore wash" their way out; founders must pick a side

**For someone based in Beijing, the opportunity landscape is exceptional.** You're at the center of the world's fastest-growing AI agent market, the embodied AI revolution, and the China-to-Global startup paradigm — with access to Shenzhen manufacturing, Tsinghua talent, and state-backed capital that doesn't exist anywhere else.

## Part 2: China's AI Ecosystem — The Full Picture

### 2.1 Market Structure

China is the world's **second-largest VC market**, with deal volume exceeding Europe by 80% in Q4'25 and nearly catching the US. Within APAC, China accounts for ~62% of all AI investment rounds.

**But the ecosystem is fundamentally different from the US:**

| Dimension | US | China |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant capital | Private VC + corporates | State-backed funds + RMB funds + CVCs |
| Key investors | Sequoia, a16z, GC | Hillhouse/GL Ventures, Oriza, state guidance funds |
| Funding focus | Models + infra + apps | Hard tech + embodied AI + chips + robotics |
| Regulatory posture | Light-touch, innovation-first | Mandatory filing, content moderation, labeling |
| Exit path | Late-stage private → IPO | Earlier IPO push (funding gap in infra) |
| Consumer AI | Open market | Big tech controls 70% of domestic Top 20 |

### 2.3 Beijing's AI Landscape

Beijing is China's **#1 AI hub**:

- **46 publicly-listed AI companies** with combined market value of $590.96B (4.3T yuan)
- **DeepSeek** became first quant hedge fund to secure 100B+ yuan in funding
- **¥20B ($2.8B) four-fund structure** launched April 2026 (integrated circuits + AI, robotics, biotech, future industries)
- **E-Town Capital** (亦庄国投) as anchor LP; EVAS got ¥1.5B Series B led by all four funds simultaneously — the day after the fund structure was announced
- **RISC-V is the chosen path** for compute independence — 8 ministries coordinated national RISC-V push in 2025
- **Entrepreneurship guarantee loans** rose 79.6% YoY in 2024
- **10 targeted initiatives** for specialized tech firms, ¥125M invested in digital upgrades for 551 companies

### 2.4 China's Regulatory Architecture for AI

China has built **the world's most detailed AI regulatory framework** by 2026:

- **346+ generative AI services filed** with CAC as of March 2025; 446 newly filed + 330 newly registered in 2025
- **Mandatory algorithm registration** before public deployment
- **AI content labeling** required — major platforms (Tencent, Douyin, RedNote, Weibo, DeepSeek) implemented compliance programs
- **Cybersecurity Law amended** October 2025 to explicitly include AI
- **Qinglang campaigns** — annual, escalating enforcement; consequences include service suspension, app store delisting, licensing revocation
- **Fines**: RMB 10K-100K for filing failures; service suspension for serious violations

**Key insight for founders**: Compliance cannot be retrofitted in China. Filing, labeling, security assessment, and content moderation must be built into the product before launch.

### 2.5 The "Singapore Washing" Crackdown

The Manus case (Meta's $2B acquisition blocked by Beijing) has **shattered the assumption** that a Singapore address provides cover for Chinese AI startups seeking Western capital:

- **Exit bans** placed on Manus founders Xiao Hong and Ji Yichao
- **MOFCOM investigating** whether core AI IP was transferred abroad without required approvals
- **MiroMind** (Chen Tianqiao) implementing strict firewalls between Chinese and overseas operations
- **New standard**: Genuine non-Chinese status requires full team relocation, customer transition, and Chinese investor exit — takes **18-24 months minimum**

**Implication**: Chinese AI founders must decide early which game they're playing — domestic (with state capital + subsidies) or global (with Western capital + exit paths). You can't easily do both anymore.

## Part 5: The China-to-Global Startup Model

### The Core Paradox

China has **270M AI users** (more than ChatGPT), yet **86% of successful Chinese AI startups now target overseas markets**. Why?

- **Big tech clones fast** — ByteDance launched 11 AI products in 2024; when Kimi's 2M-character context window went viral, Alibaba and Baidu matched it within 5 days
- **Distribution advantage** — Big tech plugs AI into existing services (Quark search, Baidu Library), slashing CAC to 1/5 of standalone apps
- **70% of domestic Top 20** controlled by big tech (ByteDance, Baidu, Alibaba, iFlytek)
- **Innovation cycles compress** — what took months now takes weeks

### The Solution: Build in China, Sell Globally

The new paradigm catalyzed by DeepSeek R1 + Manus:

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founding teams** | Chinese (Tsinghua, PKU, Big AI Lab backgrounds) |
| **Target market** | Global from Day 1, not just Chinese customers |
| **GTM** | Products and strategies oriented overseas |
| **Cost advantage** | Leverage China's engineering talent for lower R&D costs and faster iteration |
| **Exit paths** | US IPO / overseas M&A (avoids China market risk) |

**Why USD funds love this**: Global markets = larger TAM + clearer exits + no China policy risk + can benchmark against SV peers while cost structure is more efficient.

### Proven Examples

- **Plaud** — AI recording pen, $100M+ ARR in 2025, sold globally from China
- **22 Chinese AI apps** in a16z's global Top 50 — 19 target overseas users
- **Qwen** adopted by Airbnb for customer service (comparable capability at lower cost)
- **Singapore's national AI program** building on Qwen3

### The Talent Return Wave

Elite AI researchers are **returning from US to China**:

- Wu Yonghui left Google DeepMind → ByteDance LLM lead
- Yao Shunyu left OpenAI → Tencent AI development
- 30+ US-based researchers relocated to China in past 12 months (vs. low single digits a year earlier)
- ~20% of Tsinghua engineering grads now apply to US PhDs (down from ~50% pre-pandemic)
